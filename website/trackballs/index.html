Supported trackballs

Glideball fully supports the wired Kensington Expert Mouse. Other Kensington trackballs work when you turn on the Beta program. Your trackpad and mice from other brands are never changed.

Which Kensington trackballs work with Glideball?

TrackballStatusNotes
Kensington Expert Mouse, wired (USB)FullThe one Glideball is built and tested on. Everything works: pointer speed, all three scroll-ring modes, all four buttons and combos.
Kensington Expert Mouse WirelessBetaIt may show up as fully supported, depending on the name it reports. If Glideball says Beta, turn on the Beta program.
Kensington SlimBladeBetaNot tuned yet. The top buttons aren't supported yet.
Kensington Orbit trackballsBetaNot tuned yet.
Other Kensington trackballs and miceBetaGlideball treats any Kensington pointing device as a Beta device.
Other brands, the trackpad, the Magic MouseNot supportedNever touched. They keep their System Settings.

What Beta means here

Glideball is built and tested on the wired Expert Mouse, so that's the one I can vouch for. Glideball still knows the other Kensington trackballs, but it won't change them unless you ask. When it sees one, it offers Turn On Beta. You can also turn on Overview › General › Beta program.

The Beta program does two things: it lets Glideball work with Kensington's other trackballs, and it gets you early builds of the app. It's set on each Mac separately and never syncs.

If you try Glideball on one of these, please tell me how it went, good or bad. That's how a model moves to Full.

What about other brands?

Glideball is only for Kensington trackballs today. Support for other brands may come later, but I'm not promising any yet. Until then, Glideball ignores them completely.

How Glideball decides

It goes by the USB vendor ID. Kensington is 047D. A device with product ID 1020, or with “Expert Mouse” in its name, counts as the Expert Mouse. Any other Kensington pointing device is a Beta device. Everything else is left alone. The rule is in DeviceSupport.swift.

The Linux and Windows previews follow the same rule.
