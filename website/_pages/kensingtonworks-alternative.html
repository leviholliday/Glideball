Why I made it

I got a Kensington Expert Mouse and installed Kensington's app and driver. It didn't do most of what I wanted, and I had a hard time finding my way around it. So I wrote my own app. That's my experience, not a review. Plenty of people use KensingtonWorks and are happy with it.

Glideball does three things well: it lets the pointer go faster than System Settings allows, it makes the scroll ring feel the way you want, and it turns the buttons into clicks, shortcuts or two- and three-button combos. It only changes your Kensington trackball. Your trackpad and other mice keep their own settings.

KensingtonWorks on macOS Tahoe

As of November 10, 2026, Kensington's KensingtonWorks page offers Mac version 3.1.18 and lists macOS 10.14 Mojave through macOS 15 Sequoia. macOS 26 Tahoe isn't on that list. The same page says KensingtonWorks is moving to a new app, Kensington Konnect, which you can try as a beta.

I haven't tested KensingtonWorks or Konnect on Tahoe myself, so I can't tell you whether they work there. Glideball needs macOS 26 Tahoe or later, and that's what I test it on.

What's different about Glideball

  • It's only for Kensington trackballs. SteerMouse and LinearMouse are good general mouse tools. Glideball is narrower on purpose: it knows the Expert Mouse's scroll ring and four buttons, and leaves every other device alone.
  • The scroll ring has three feels. Flywheel (the default) pushes the page with each tick and lets friction slow it down. Follow tracks the ring exactly. Native lets macOS do it, with a faster wheel speed for the trackball only.
  • No driver. Glideball never takes the trackball away from macOS. If you pause or quit it, the trackball is an ordinary mouse again. ⌃⌥⌘G pauses it from anywhere, so a bad mapping can't lock you out.
  • You can read all of it. The code is on GitHub under the MIT license. The app has no analytics. See Privacy.

What it doesn't do: it can't import your KensingtonWorks settings, it isn't notarized by Apple (so the first launch needs one extra click), and only the wired Expert Mouse is fully supported today. Other Kensington trackballs work through the Beta program. See supported trackballs.

Switch in five minutes

  1. Uninstall KensingtonWorks and restart

    Its driver and Glideball both try to handle the buttons, so you'd get double actions. How to uninstall KensingtonWorks.

  2. Download Glideball

    Unzip Glideball.zip and drag Glideball into Applications. Or use Homebrew:

    brew install --cask leviholliday/tap/glideball

  3. Open it once with Open Anyway

    macOS will say it couldn't verify Glideball. Click Done, then System Settings › Privacy & Security › Open Anyway. Step by step.

  4. Allow two permissions

    Accessibility and Input Monitoring. Glideball's window has a button for each. What they let it see.

  5. Set up your buttons

    On the Buttons tab, use Quick assign: press the buttons, then the shortcut. Then try the scroll modes on the Scrolling tab.

Questions

Is there a free alternative to KensingtonWorks for Mac?

Yes. Glideball is free and open source (MIT). It works with Kensington trackballs on macOS 26 Tahoe or later. LinearMouse is another free, open-source option for mice in general.

Can I keep KensingtonWorks installed too?

No. Uninstall it and restart first. With both running, buttons can do two things at once.

Can Glideball import my KensingtonWorks settings?

No. Quick assign on the Buttons tab makes setting things up again fast: press the buttons, then the shortcut you want.

Does it work with the SlimBlade or the Orbit?

Through the Beta program, yes, but I haven't tuned Glideball on them. The wired Expert Mouse is the one that's fully supported. See supported trackballs.

Who makes Glideball?

I do. I'm Levi Holliday, a student. Glideball is an independent project and isn't connected to Kensington.
