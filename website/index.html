Glideball is a free Mac app for Kensington trackballs. It lets the pointer go faster than macOS allows, makes the scroll ring scroll smoothly, and lets you choose what each button does. There's no driver to install and no account to make.

  • 0 drivers to install macOS still moves the cursor; Glideball just adjusts it.
  • $0 free and open source (MIT) No account to make.
  • 1 shortcut to pause it ⌃⌥⌘GControl Option Command G pauses it from anywhere.
  • 9 languages It follows your Mac's language.

Apple hasn't notarized Glideball, so you'll approve it once in System Settings. Here's how. It's built on the wired Kensington Expert Mouse, and other Kensington trackballs work if you turn on Other Kensington trackballs in Settings.

    Ball

    A faster trackball pointer than macOS allows.

    The tracking speed slider in System Settings stops at 3. Glideball goes way past that, so one flick of the ball gets you across a big display, and slow rolls are still precise.

    • Only your trackball. Your trackpad and other mice keep their own settings.
    • No added lag. macOS still moves the cursor; Glideball just changes the curve.
    • Presets or fine control. Pick Precise, macOS, Fast or Turbo, or set it anywhere in between.

    Ring

    Make the Kensington scroll ring feel the way you want.

    There are three scroll modes, and you can switch between them any time. They only change your trackball, not your trackpad or other mice.

    • Native. macOS scrolls the ring the way it always has, and you can turn the wheel speed up for the trackball alone.
    • Flywheel. Kensington's feel Each notch gives the page a push and friction slows it to a stop, so gentle turns barely coast and hard spins go a long way. I based it on measurements of Kensington's own driver, and it runs at your display's refresh rate.
    • Follow. The page follows the ring notch for notch and stops when you stop. If you want, a real flick can still coast.

    Buttons

    Remap the buttons, or press two at once for something else.

    Each button can be a click, a keyboard shortcut, Mission Control, a Space switch and more. Press two or three together and that combo gets its own action too. Setting one up takes two presses.

    • Quick assign. Press a trackball button, then the shortcut you want it to send. That's it.
    • Two- and three-button combos. Press buttons together and they do their own thing, on top of what each one does alone.
    • Your main click stays put. The bottom-left button is always a left click, so you can't lock yourself out.

    Overview

    See what your trackball is doing, live.

    The Overview tab graphs ball speed and scroll-ring activity as they happen, lights up each button as you press it, and keeps a tally of your day: clicks, distance rolled, distance scrolled.

    A different setup for every app.

    Pick an app and give it its own pointer speed, scrolling, buttons and combos. Glideball switches when that app comes to the front, and switches back when you leave it.

    Precision

    Slows the pointer down for fine work. Hold a button for it, or press once to turn it on and again to turn it off.

    Scroll with ball

    Roll the ball to scroll in any direction, sideways too. The pointer stays where it is.

    Drag lock

    Holds the click down for you, so you can drag without keeping a finger on the button. Click again to let go.

    You can put each of these on a button or a keyboard shortcut.

    The same setup on every Mac you use.

    Turn on sync and your other Macs get the same setup through your own iCloud Drive, with no account to make. You can also export your settings to a file and import them on another Mac.

    Glideball backs up your settings on its own too: every day for two weeks, then once a month for a year. So if you change something and don't like it, you can go back with one click.

    Is Glideball safe to use?

    I built it so a bad setting can't leave you stuck, and so your trackball goes back to normal whenever Glideball isn't running.

    Instant pause ⌃⌥⌘G

    Press Control Option Command G⌃⌥⌘G from anywhere and Glideball steps aside. Press it again to turn it back on.

    Fails safe

    If you pause or quit Glideball, or it doesn't have permission, your buttons and scrolling work the way macOS normally does.

    No driver

    It's a regular app, not a driver or system extension. macOS keeps control of the trackball the whole time.

    Open source

    MIT licensed. You can read every line on GitHub.

    Private

    The app has no tracking. It sends crash reports or stats only if you say yes, and otherwise only goes online to check GitHub for updates, to sync through your own iCloud Drive if you turn that on, and to send feedback when you press Send.

    Checked updates

    Updates install in one click. Glideball won't install a download that isn't signed with the same certificate as the app you already have.

    How to install Glideball

    Glideball isn't on the App Store, so the first launch takes one extra step. Here's exactly what you'll see.

    1. Remove KensingtonWorks Do this first

      Uninstall KensingtonWorks, then restart your Mac once so its driver lets go of the trackball. The two can't run together.

    2. Download

      Download Glideball.zip, unzip it, and drag Glideball into your Applications folder.

      Or use Homebrew: brew install --cask leviholliday/tap/glideball

    3. Open it once and approve it

      Open Glideball. macOS will say “Glideball” Not Opened. Click Done, then Open Anyway in System Settings › Privacy & Security:

      1. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security and scroll down to Security.
      2. Next to the message about Glideball, click Open Anyway. The button shows for about an hour after you tried to open the app. If it's gone, open Glideball again.
      3. Enter your password or use Touch ID, then click Open Anyway one more time.

      You only do this once. Control-click › Open doesn't work for this anymore on macOS 15 and later.

    4. Allow two permissions

      Glideball's window has a button for each. Until both are on, your trackball just works normally.

      • Input Monitoring lets Glideball read your trackball: its buttons, its ball and its scroll ring.
      • Accessibility lets Glideball change what those buttons and scrolls do.

      What it can't see: it never monitors your typing. It only sees mouse buttons, scrolling, and which modifier keys (⌘ ⌥ ⌃ ⇧) are held, plus the keys you press while recording a shortcut in Glideball's own window. None of that leaves your Mac.

    Questions about Glideball

    Is there a KensingtonWorks alternative for Mac?

    Yes. Glideball is a free, open-source Mac app that replaces KensingtonWorks for Kensington trackballs on macOS 26 Tahoe and later. It covers pointer speed, scroll-ring scrolling, button remapping and two- or three-button combos, and adds per-app setups and a live dashboard. Uninstall KensingtonWorks before you install it.

    Which Kensington trackballs does Glideball support?

    Glideball fully supports the wired Kensington Expert Mouse. Other Kensington trackballs, like the Expert Mouse Wireless, SlimBlade and Orbit, work when you turn on Other Kensington trackballs in Glideball › Settings › General, but I haven't tuned Glideball on them yet, so you may hit bugs. Glideball never changes other brands' mice or your trackpad.

    How do I make the Kensington scroll ring scroll smoothly on a Mac?

    Install Glideball and pick the Flywheel scroll mode. Each notch of the ring gives the page a push that glides to a stop, based on measurements of Kensington's own driver. If you'd rather have the page follow the ring exactly, pick Follow.

    Can I make my trackball pointer faster than macOS allows?

    Yes. The tracking speed slider in System Settings stops at 3. Glideball lets your Kensington trackball go well past that, and slow movements stay precise. Your trackpad and other mice aren't affected.

    Why does macOS say Glideball can't be opened? Is it safe?

    Apple hasn't notarized Glideball, because notarizing needs a paid Apple Developer membership ($99 a year). macOS blocks the first launch of any app like that. To open it, click Done, then Open Anyway in System Settings › Privacy & Security. Glideball is open source, so anyone can read every line on GitHub.

    Do I need to uninstall KensingtonWorks first?

    Yes. Uninstall KensingtonWorks and restart your Mac once before you install Glideball. Kensington's driver handles the trackball itself, and the two conflict.

    What can Glideball see with Accessibility and Input Monitoring?

    Glideball never monitors your typing. It only sees mouse buttons, scrolling and which modifier keys are held, plus the keys you press while recording a shortcut in its own window. Input Monitoring lets it read the trackball. Accessibility lets it change what the buttons and scrolling do.

    Does Glideball collect my data?

    Not unless you say yes. The app has no tracking and no account. The first time you open it, it asks whether it can send me crash reports, or crash reports and anonymous settings stats, and it's off until you pick one. Besides that, it only goes online to check GitHub for updates, to sync through your own iCloud Drive if you turn that on, and to send feedback when you press Send, and you can see exactly what's included before it goes. This website is separate: it can use optional analytics, which the website privacy notice at the bottom of the page explains.

    What do the optional crash reports and stats send?

    A crash report says where in Glideball's code it crashed, plus your Glideball and macOS versions. Stats, sent once a day, say which Kensington trackball you have and a few settings, like the scroll mode and roughly how fast the pointer is. Neither one includes what you type, which apps you use or your name. You can change your choice, or delete what was sent, any time in Glideball › Settings › Privacy. The privacy page lists everything that's sent.

    Does it work on macOS 15 Sequoia or older?

    No. Glideball needs macOS 26 Tahoe or later, on Apple silicon or Intel. Linux and Windows versions are available as a preview.

    How do I pause or uninstall Glideball?

    Press ⌃⌥⌘G (Control-Option-Command-G) from anywhere to pause it, and your buttons and scrolling go back to normal. To uninstall, quit Glideball and drag it to the Trash, or run brew uninstall --zap --cask glideball if you used Homebrew. To remove everything by hand, also delete ~/Library/Application Support/Glide, ~/Library/Logs/Glide and the Glide folder in iCloud Drive, run defaults delete com.leviholliday.glide, and remove Glideball from the Accessibility and Input Monitoring lists in System Settings › Privacy & Security.

    Is Glideball available in my language?

    Glideball speaks English, Español, Français, Deutsch, Italiano, Português (Brasil), 日本語, 한국어 and 简体中文. It follows your Mac's language, or you can pick one in Glideball › Settings › General › Language.
